Consumer Confidence:
    Consumer Confidence affects target spending
    If pops overspend -> accumulate debt

    110 confidence:

    1000 income -> 1100 spending
    100 debt accumulation
    10% interest?

    After 1 yr: 1000 debt, 100 yrly debt payment, ~8 per month

    Under 100 confidence, underspending, results in paying off debt
    Higher interest payment -> decreases consumer confidence
        % of income that is debt payment reduces cons confidence by that amount?
    
    8 per month means 0.8 consumer confidence reduction
        this means 8 of target spending is spent on interest and target spending gets reduced by 8 as well
        so spending goes down by about 1.6%

    If pops are under 100 CC, they will start withdrawing from savings instead of borrowing money
    Reducing SoL due to overspending takes longer than increasing SoL

    If pops cannot borrow/withdraw, then their target spending & SoL will drop like a rock

Savings
    Banking Confidence determines the excess cash (that gets dumped into increasing SoL) that gets put into savings
    Savings can be withdrawn in times of stress

    Building cash reserves also count as savings
    Investment Fund can also dump excess cash into the bank
    
    All sources of savings earn money from interest on debt currently lent out

    Pops will begin withdrawing from savings & reduce debt when CC dips below 100, otherwise they will borrow money. 
    IFs will begin withdrawing from savings & reduce debt when IC dips below 100, otherwise they will borrow money.

    12% savings will be withdrawn per year (1% per month)

State Banks
    Pops' and Buildings' savings will accumulate in state banks
    Investment Fund savings will accumulate in the national bank

    Pops & Buildings will borrow from state banks

    Disabled by default

National Bank
    Countries borrow from the national bank. It's an accumulation of the state banks (for ease of calculation)

    If a state doesn't have a state bank, then a penalty is applied to the amount transferred over to the national level

Central Banking
    Only available if you have Private/Public Central Banking or Nationalized Banking

    Having a central bank will allow state banks to borrow from the central bank to correct pitfalls
    Also allows setting the interest rate for domestic borrowing (which affects spending)
    State banks will borrow money until their interest rate is just above the national rate
        They will pay back money if it's below that


Credit Rating
    Risk of lending to a particular group/individual
    Each potential borrower is given a credit rating (AAA to SD/D) (100.0-0.0) to assess risk
        >=90 = AAA
        >=80 = AA
        >=70 = A
        >=60 = BBB
        >=50 = BB
        >=40 = B
        >=30 = CCC
        >=20 = CC
        >=10 = C
        <=00 = SD/D

    Based upon earnings (with different weights given to various sources), current interest payment % of modified revenue, 
    current available credit, current spending (modified based on sources. More weight given to permanent spending than temporary)
        Power ranking also affects this. Regionals & Great Powers get higher scores automatically

    Higher risk = higher interest rate

    For countries, higher credit ratings allow greater & cheaper access to borrowing from foreign nations
    It essentially will provide a cap, probably based on a function of (solid) revenue

    For pops/investment fund, credit ratings affect behavior. 
    Low credit ratings (below A) will spur pops to cut spending, or even make withdrawals (below B)
    Same with investment funds

Bank Run
    The higher your banking confidence, the harder this will hit
    
    If the bank isn't bailed out, it fails. This can cause a chain of failures
        Enable like a panic modifier

    Tanks your banking confidence, increases interest rates

Default
    A state any borrower can go in. It means that they have reached the maximum amount they can borrow and cannot
    borrow any further.

    For countries, it will give a scaling modifier which will be more ruinous the less you can fund your govt
        and an event every month you are in default

    Bankruptcy can then follow. It will wipe the debts & the savings attached to that debt.
    For countries, it will give a nasty modifier.

    A country can only go bankrupt if it's below a B rating

Land & Housing
	Arable Land
		1 Arable Land = 25,000 acres of usable farming land, enough for 1000 subsistence farmers & families to comfortably live off of
		
		Available Land
			Land that is available for subsistence farming
			Can be converted into Industrial Land or Urbanized Land via land purchases
		Forested Land
			Can be used to produce wood
			Can be sustained, deforested, or reforested
				Deforestation - High wood output, forest size decreases, available land increases, attractive when land value is high
				Sustainment - Stable forest size, wood output capped by forest size
				Reforestation - Low wood output, forest size increases, available land decreases
				Decision for which policy is made at the state level, but can be influenced by national policies
		Industrial Land
			Land used by rural industries (large-scale ag)
			Consumes land while active
			Assumed to be owned by the industry
		Urbanized Land
			Land used for housing and urban infrastructure
			Consumes land while active
			Ownership depends on housing ownership
		
	Land Ownership
		Affects available and forested land

		Smallholders
			Represents peasant ownership
		Landowners
			Represents estates, landlords, etc.
		State
			May own land directly
	
	How this affects Subsistence Farming:
		Peasants will, by default, work on smallholder land first
		If smallholder land is exhausted, they will work on land owned by landowners
			Smallholders will then be paid wages by landowners
		
		State-owned land priority will depend on policy
			If state land provides subsidies to subsistence farming, peasants will work on it after smallholder land
			If not, it will be worked on last
		
		If there are more peasants than available smallholder land, wages will drop significantly
	
	Subsistence Farm Output:
		Instead of dropping by half after available land is exhausted, output will slowly drop with saturation according to the following formula:
		f(l) = W * (l / l + h)
		where l = land per worker, W = max output per worker, h = half-saturation constant
		This means that output will drop off more slowly as land becomes scarce, but will still decrease
		by default, h = 0.75 (meaning output is half of max when land per worker is 0.75) 
	
	Land Purchase
		Order of acquisition:
			1. The State (if it is selling land)
			2. Landowners (only if they have excess land)
			3. Smallholders
		
		Land Value =
			(Base Land Value) 
			* (Urbanized + Industrial Land Share) 
			* (Rural Pop / Available Land) 
			* (Average Income)

		Destruction of industries will result in land either being given to the state, to smallholders, or landowners based on policy

	Housing
		Each state tracks:
			Housing Capacity
			Housing Demand
			Occupancy Ratio
			Rent Price
		
		Housing Demand
			Calculated per pop type based on SoL
			Occupancy ratio = Housing Demand / Housing Capacity
			Pops pay rent to landowners
		
		Rent Price =
			Base Rent * f(Occupancy Ratio)
			where f(x) = 1 + (x - 1)^2 for x > 1, and f(x) = 1 / (2 - x) for x < 1
		
		Housing Ownership
			Landowners own housing by default
			The State may also own housing

			State owned housing functions as a discount for pops
				Priority depends on policy (e.g. soldiers->bureaucrats->poorest)
				Eligible pops get a rent discount based on state ownership percentage
				Lost rent is assumed to be absorbed by the state as a subsidy
			
	Housing Construction
		Housing can be constructed by:
			Landowners
			The State (if state housing policy is active)
		
		Two construction modes:
			Build Out
				consumes available land - must buy land
				Cannot build if there is no available land to be bought
				Decreases cost of future build-ups
			Build Up
				Higher cost and increases cost of future build-ups
		
	Housing Deprecation
		Housing deprecates over time, reducing capacity

		Housing Capacity decreases by 0.2% per month

		Housing maintenance is represented through deprecation

Spreadsheet shenanigans
	Automation Method desired roi: 5 years

	Wage Mult of production method: E
	Wage Mult Old: W
	Wage Mult New: N
	Construction Cost: C
	Value Add difference: V (negative if new costs more to operate)

	R = (W-N) / (E+W) = Ratio of wage savings to current wages
	Monthly Wage Savings = R * Current Wages

	ROI in years = C / (Monthly Wage Savings + V) / 12.0

	Example:
		Production Method:
			Revenue: 15.0
			Inputs: 5.0
			Wages: 9.0
			Wage Mult: 4.0

		Old Automation Method:
			Wage Mult: -2.0
			Value Add: -0.5
		
		New Automation Method:
			Wage Mult: -3.0
			Value Add: -1.0
			Cost: 20.0
		W = -2.0
		N = -3.0
		E = 4.0
		C = 20.0
		V = -1.0 - (-0.5) = -0.5
		R = (-2 - -3) / (4 + -2) = 1/2 = 0.5
		Monthly Wage Savings = 0.5 * 9.0 = 4.5
		ROI = 20.0 / (4.5 + -0.5) / 12.0 = 20.0 / 4.0 / 12.0 = 5 months / 12.0 = 0.417 years
