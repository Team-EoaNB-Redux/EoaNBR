# India c.1857 — Industry Data

Oct 31, 2026 · @Someone

India in 1857 had about 239 million people, roughly 8½ times the UK's population, but an economy perhaps two-thirds the UK's in nominal money. About two-thirds of output came from agriculture, a fifth from (mostly handicraft) industry and a tenth from services. Its foreign trade, about £26.6m of exports, was a fifth of Britain's. It ran a merchandise surplus that was settled in imported silver: £14.4m of treasure came in during 1857.

India's statistics are much thinner than Britain's. There was no census until 1867–72, no regular crop returns until the 1880s, and most production was village-level and never measured. External trade (customs) and government monopolies (opium, salt) are the firm numbers. Almost everything else is an estimate.

## How to read this

- Basis tags are the same as in the British Isles doc. **S** = checked against a linked source this session. **H** = standard historical figure, recalled. **E** = my estimate. Expect far more E here than in the British doc.
- Currency: Rs 10 = £1, the conversion used in the period trade tables (S). 1 crore = Rs 10m = £1m. 1 lakh = Rs 100,000 = £10,000. A maund is about 80 lb (the Bengal bazaar maund is 82⅔ lb).
- Trade years: the trade figures come from R. C. Dutt's tables, which label years singly ("1857"). The Indian official year then ran from May to April, so "1857" may mean 1856–57. The page does not say (S).
- 1857 itself was abnormal. The Rebellion (May 1857–Nov 1858) wrecked output in the North-Western Provinces, Oudh, Delhi, Bundelkhand and parts of Central India; deaths probably reached about 800,000, mostly from famine and disease. For a balanced "normal" baseline, 1855–56 is safer than 1857–58.

### Scale of the economy

- Sectoral shares of output c.1871: agriculture 67.5%; industry and commerce 22.2% (domestic industry 21.5%, export industry 0.7%); services and housing 10.3% (S).
- Real GDP per head was about $586 (1990 international dollars) in 1851 and $554 in 1861, roughly a fifth of Britain's (21.6% in 1851, 18.1% in 1861) (S). About $570 is a sensible 1857 value (E).
- In money of the day, national income was roughly Rs 20 a head (Dadabhai Naoroji's later estimate for British India, H), so about £450–500m for all India (E). For comparison, the UK was about £21 a head.
- Government of India gross revenue in 1856–57 was about £33m (H): land revenue \~£17–18m, opium \~£5m, salt \~£2.5m, customs \~£1.5m.
- The labour force was perhaps 95–105m (E): roughly 70% agriculture, 15% industry (mostly part-time and household), 15% services, trade and military.

## Resource industries

Modern extraction in India in 1857 meant only the Raniganj coalfield, at about 200,000–250,000 tons a year. Metals were still smelted and worked village by village. India was a net importer of every metal except some iron, and absorbed vast amounts of silver and gold as bullion. Timber and salt were the big resource trades. Salt was a government monopoly in all three presidencies.

## Agricultural industries

Agriculture produced about two-thirds of Indian output, roughly £300–330m a year (E), and employed perhaps 70m people. Almost all of it was grain eaten in the village where it grew. The cash crops that reached world markets were opium (£7.06m exported in 1857), raw cotton, indigo, sugar, grain, silk, jute and wool. Together they still made up only a few percent of farm output (S for export values).

## Manufacturing

Indian manufacturing in 1857 was almost entirely household and artisan work, and still large: about a fifth of all output (S) and perhaps 13–15m workers, many of them part-time (E). Handlooms wove about 1.55 billion yards of cotton cloth a year, against roughly 430 million yards imported from Britain (S-derived). Factory industry was a handful of firms: three or so cotton mills in Bombay and Broach, one jute mill at Rishra, government arsenals, and railway and dockyard workshops.

### Who runs it, for game purposes

- **Indian capital:** Parsi, Gujarati Bania, Bhatia and Marwari merchants run Bombay cotton mills, shipping, opium brokerage, banking (shroffs) and the inland grain and cotton trade.
- **British agency houses:** Calcutta (Jardine Skinner, Carr Tagore's successors) and Bombay firms run indigo, coal, tea, jute and import-export.
- **The state (EIC, then the Crown from Nov 1858):** monopolises opium and salt, and runs arsenals, gun-carriage factories and the Bombay Dockyard.

## Intangibles

Services and housing produced only about 10% of Indian output c.1871 (S), roughly £45–50m in 1857 (E), but employed perhaps 12–15m people. Much of that was village service castes paid in grain, plus traders, moneylenders, boatmen, carters and the army. Entertainment ran on patronage. The princely and Mughal courts were its bankers, and 1856–58 destroyed two of the greatest, Lucknow and Delhi.

## Trade totals, 1857

India exported £26.6m and imported £14.2m of merchandise in 1857, a visible surplus of £12.4m. It also imported £14.4m of treasure, mostly silver (S). Silver arrived to pay for Indian exports, especially opium sold to China. That inflow outweighed the sums India remitted to London for the "Home Charges" (H: \~£3m a year), plus capital raised in London for the guaranteed railways (H). Because so much of the surplus came back as bullion and not as goods, India looks like a giant silver sink in the game.

### Trading partners

- **United Kingdom:** in 1856–58, India took £14–18m a year of imports from Britain (including treasure routed through London) and sent about £10m of exports there. Britain's share was a little over half of Indian trade (S). The British side of the ledger recorded £21.1m of imports from "India, Ceylon & Singapore" in 1857 (see the British Isles doc). The gap reflects Ceylon, the Straits and different valuation.
- **China:** almost all the opium (\~£7m) plus some raw cotton (E). China pays in silver, which flows on to India and to London.
- **Others (E):** the Straits and Southeast Asia (rice, piece goods); the Persian Gulf, Arabia and East Africa (grain, cloth, from Bombay); France and the US (indigo, saltpetre, jute, hides); Mauritius and Australia (rice, sugar, indentured labour).
