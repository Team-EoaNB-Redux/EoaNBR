Onboarding for coding agents (Codex reads this file; Claude Code reads it through `CLAUDE.md`). Keep it current: if you change how the economy behaves, update this file and `toi/documentation/economy_overview.md` in the same change.

## What this repo is
- **End of a New Beginning Redux**, a Hearts of Iron IV total-conversion mod with an 1857 start. The mod root is `toi/` (descriptor `toi.mod`), laid out like any HOI4 mod: `common/`, `events/`, `history/`, `interface/`, `localisation/`, `gfx/`, `map/`.
- Most agent work here is on the **custom economy**, led by Strat (GitHub `Strategy-Gamer`). Focus trees, events and the map are ordinary HOI4 modding.
- There's no build step. Testing means loading the game, which is slow (saves take 5–10 minutes to load), or running the headless simulator (see Testing).

## The monthly tick (`_advance_time`, then `_country_advance_time` per country in rank order)
First, every market leader runs `market_prices_monthly`: actual prices step toward the targets the last weekly tick set, are copied to members and states, and `market_cache_update` refreshes the PM/AM cache. Then, per country: `national_debt_update` → `add_treasury` (net budget) → `ai_budget` (AI only) → `spending_effects_update` → `update_national_production_methods` → `r_update_modifiers_country_effects` → `bureaucracy_and_manpower_update` → `sectors_monthly` (sector capital, target utilisation) → **per controlled state:** `on_monthly_industries` → `on_monthly_pops` (finances, growth, slaves, active manpower, qualifications, peasant migration) → `base_wage_update` → `industries_monthly` (trailing margins, decay) → `pops_balance` → `fix_negative_workforce` → `reload_industries_array` → `subsist_and_arable_update` → `employment_update` → `govt_building_update` → `industries_update_construction_retooling` → `industries_update` → `pops_update` → **country:** `national_market_states_update` (goods totals) → `sectors_update` → (gated) `io_*` → `companies_monthly` → `companies_update` → `investment_fund_update` → (gated) `investment_funds_monthly` / `investment_opportunities_monthly` / `national_opportunities_monthly` → `update_country_pop_stats` → market leader: `calc_shipping_capacity`, `market_update` (from the members' cached goods totals), `market_statistics_update`, `market_calc_gdp` → `budget_update` → `national_debt_update` → statistics → `check_economic_cycle`.

The one-tick lags are intentional: wages use last tick's labour utilisation, target utilisation last tick's sector margins, dividends last tick's sector accounts, revenue the price set at the start of the tick from last month's supply and demand.

## Data model
- **Tokens** index everything: `token:food`, `token:working_class`, `token:steel_foundry`, `token:pm_x`. Per-token variables are written `var@token:food` (a literal token) or `var@var:t` (a token held in a variable).
- **Static definitions** are globals: `global.base_price@token:food`, `global.pm_output_amount@var:pm`, `global.building_base_employees@var:b`.
- **Per-industry data lives on the state**, keyed by the building token, and holds only what calculations need (M04, Q90): `size@var:bu_token`, PM and AM, construction and retooling, ownership, jobs and employment per class, `output@` (bundles of base production made) and `input_scale@`, the money the accounts add up (`revenue@`, `expenses_input@`, `expenses_payroll@`, `expenses_maintenance@`), `dividends@` and the trailing `margin@`. Tooltips work out the rest through the `get_industry_*` getters. States keep no per-good figures except their pops' demand (`pops_need@`). State arrays: `industries` (everything, including RGOs and special buildings), `buildings`, `rgos`.
- **Land** (M05) lives on the state: `arable_land`, `arable_land_available` (what commercial farms leave), `land_quality`, `crop_eligible@<farm>`, `subsistence_capacity` (K peasants) and `peasants_marginal_land`. The subsistence farm keeps `size@` (the land left), `output@`, `revenue@`, `expenses_payroll@` (the peasants' share), `dividends@` (rent), `owner_jobs@` and `productivity@`.
- **Deposits** (M06) live on the state, per RGO good: `known_deposits@` (found, worked or not), `potential_deposits@` (not found yet) and `deposit_quality@` (1 unless history sets it). The worked deposit is the RGO's `size@` (Q68), never above its known deposits, and quality multiplies the RGO's output only (Q98). RGOs are the natural goods (`global.rgo_token_array`: wood, coal, iron, copper, valuables, oil, rubber, aluminum, tungsten, chromium, titanium, silicon, rare earths, chemicals, food, fiber, luxuries); manufactured goods have only factories (Q97). No economy code reads HOI4 state resources.
- **Sectors** (M04) live on the country, keyed by the industry token: `sector_size@`, `sector_revenue@`, `sector_profit@`, `sector_margin@`, `sector_capital@`, `sector_dividend_per_size@`, `sector_utilisation@` (target utilisation, %), `sector_states@` (its five biggest states), jobs and employment per class, transport, construction, retooling and reserves (Q91), and the rest (`sectors_update`, `sectors_monthly`). The country's `sectors` and `methods_in_use` arrays list what its state-industries use. Its goods come from the sectors (`national_market_states_update`): `output@<sector>`, and the method mix in arrays parallel to `global.building_production_methods@<sector>` and `building_automation_methods@`, `sector_pm_scale@<sector>^i` / `sector_am_scale@` (input scale) and `sector_pm_size@` / `sector_am_size@` (size). The PM/AM cache holds `market_output_value@<sector>` (the value of one bundle), `method_input_cost@<method>` and `method_construction_cost@<method>` on every country, copied from its market leader.
- **Prices** sit on the market leader, are copied to every member country and state, and are read as `controller:price@var:r`. `price@` is the **actual** price, which money uses and which moves once a month. `target_price@` is supply and demand's target (`get_price`), recomputed weekly, and the trade AI reads it. World prices work the same way: `global_market_price@` (actual) and `global_market_target_price@` (target), per market leader. The shipping price, `price_shipping_capacity`, is also set on the market leader, monthly, from its shipping usage (M07), and copied to members.
- **Pops** (M02): five classes in `global.pops_array`: `slaves`, `peasants`, `working_class`, `middle_class`, `upper_class`. Each state holds `pops_workforce@token:<class>` and the rest per class. Soldiers sit in the state's `active_manpower`, outside the classes, so the classes plus `active_manpower` add up to `pops_workforce`. Industries offer `working_jobs@` and `middle_jobs@` (the sheet's Wrk and Mid) and `owner_jobs@` for the upper class.
- **Units:** pops in thousands. Money: 1,000 = £1M. Every flow is per month.
- **Temps don't belong to a scope.** A temp keeps its value across scope changes within one effect execution and is cleared when that execution ends. Set a temp, change scope, read it. Don't build `PREV.` plumbing for it.

## Script semantics (confirmed in-game or by the simulator)
- `check_variable` has no `<=` or `>=`. Use `<`, `>`, `=` and `NOT`. Negative literals compare fine (`> -1`).
- Multiply and divide truncate. Dividing by 0 gives 0 in plain variable effects, but **a math expression doesn't** guard against it, so guard it yourself. Raw × raw wraps in 64 bits, so a product above roughly 922M goes wrong.
- **Math expressions** (`set_variable = { x = { value = a multiply = b ... } }`) are preferred for formulas because they're faster. Rules:
  - Globals, normal variables and `@token:` / `@var:` reads work directly inside them.
  - Stage `arr^i` into a temp first.
  - Keep math expressions out of trigger blocks (`all_of`, the getters). Use plain temp effects there.
  - They aren't allowed in `modulo_variable` or `clamp_variable`; use `mod` / `clamp` inside the expression instead.
  - A parse failure silently evaluates to 0.
  - **A load error earlier in the same file zeroes every later math expression in it.** That covers a syntax error, an unknown effect or trigger, a rejected expression, even `log = ""`. This is how M02 first loaded with everyone a peasant. The game logs each zeroed expression in `error.log` as "Errors occurred while reading math expression defaulting to 0". The simulator reports the cause as `load_error`, and zeroes the same expressions.
  - `clamp` takes `min` first: `clamp = { min = 0 max = 1 }`. Max first, or max alone, is a load error.
- `clamp_variable` / `clamp_temp_variable` with crossed bounds (min above max) give the min, in either order. The simulator models this (`pops_handle_savings` relied on it until M03 removed savings).
- The getters are triggers because the GUI and scripted localisation call them, and effects aren't allowed there. Keep them trigger-safe.
- **A console probe isn't a tick.** Some reads return 0 from the console but work in ticks. Judge in-game behaviour from a save, not the console.

## Conventions (Strat's)
- `*_update` and `calculate_*` effects are **idempotent**: they recompute from scratch with `set_*`. `*_monthly` effects **advance state**, run exactly once per tick, and run before the matching `_update`.
- Document temp inputs and outputs in a header comment (`# x_temp - amount`, `# Output: borrow_temp_k`).
- Content (events, focuses, decisions) calls the documented wrappers (`add_x_industries`, `construct_x_industries`, `add_treasury`, `add_debt`, `borrow_money`, ...), never the internals.
- **Temps are for values read many times.** Don't stage a single-use value into a temp; read the source directly.
- **Set the target directly.** Don't add a tracking variable plus a lookup table when the final value can be set in history or in an existing effect.
- Match the surrounding style and comment density, and write comments in plain English. The files mix tabs and spaces, so copy exact lines before an exact-match edit.
- Ask Strat before adding a new file to `toi/`, debug and probe files included.

## Testing and verification
Failures are silent, so "it loads" proves nothing. After editing economy script:
1. If `redux-eco-sim/` exists, work from inside it:
   - `bin/ecosim_cli.exe check --parse-only` finds brace and syntax errors across the mod in under a second.
   - `bin/ecosim_cli.exe run --scenario scenarios/world_1857_eng.txt --months 12 --out runs/<name>`, then read `runs/<name>/report.md` first. Any `load_error` there means a file's math expressions read 0 in-game.
   - For an A/B test, make a baseline run before the edit, then `bin/ecosim_cli.exe compare runs/<baseline> runs/<name>`.
   - The simulator interprets the real scripts and matches in-game British GDP to about 0.5%.
2. After any PM or sheet change, run `verify_pm_audit.py`. It needs the simulator binary.
3. For real in-game state, run `python redux-eco-sim/tools/read_save.py --country ENG` (or `--state <id>`, `--var ENG.gdp_amount`). It reads the newest autosave. **Check a save before believing a theory.**
4. Compare against saves only on `world_1857_eng.txt`, and only saves made on the current code. Strat's test games are on Easy difficulty.
5. After an in-game load, search `Documents/Paradox Interactive/Hearts of Iron IV/logs/error.log` for `Errors occurred while reading math expression`. Each hit is an expression that reads 0, and the first other error above it in the same file is the cause.

The full tools table is in `economy_rework/00_README.md`.

- **Pops (M02):** five classes. One floating wage per state, with the working class at 2×, the middle class at 4×, and the upper class at 10× for senior government jobs only (owners live on dividends). Moves into the middle and upper classes need free `qualifications`, one stock per state. Slaves come only from history's `slave_share` (test blocks in Virginia, South Carolina and Mississippi); they work unpaid and count in the labour supply. Bureaucratic jobs come from population, are paid through `administration_spending`, and set `administrative_capacity`, which scales tax collection. Active manpower is HOI4's fielded and training manpower, taken out of the peasants and the working class. The game reads laws only through modifiers (`slavery_banned` and the qualification factors), and no history sets the new laws yet.
- **Wealth and demand (M03):** SoL is renamed wealth (`pops_wealth@<class>`, 1–200), with the same baskets (`global.pops_need_at_wealth@<good>^<level>`). Pops hold no savings. `pops_earnings` is gross income, and income tax is charged on it. The middle and upper classes then invest a share of their after-tax income (`middle_class_investment_contribution_factor`, base 0.1, and `upper_class_investment_contribution_factor`, base 0.2). Target spending = (earnings − investment) × consumer confidence, and taxes and debt repayments count as spending. `pops_debt_monthly` turns spending above income into `pops_debt@<class>` for the working, middle and upper classes (`global.pops_borrows@`); they repay 4% of it a year (`global.pops_debt_repayment_rate`), and half of any surplus repays more. `pops_wealth_monthly` moves wealth toward the level where spending meets the target (`global.pops_wealth_speed`). Peasants can't borrow, so their target is capped at their income. Subsistence owner jobs are 2% of all the state's peasants, and startup takes the landowners out of the peasants after the other classes.
- **Land and subsistence (M05):** arable land comes from history, falling back to `pops_workforce / 15 + 1` (Q24, Q93). `land_quality` (1 unless history sets it) scales carrying capacity and every farm's output (Q95). `crop_eligible@<farm>` blocks building farms where the crop can't grow: crop farms and livestock ranches are common (`global.building_common_crop@`), and the rest grow only where history allows them (Q63, Q71). No history sets quality or eligibility yet. Subsistence: `subsistence_capacity` = land left × quality × (1 + the PM throughput factor of `pm_subsistence_farming`, which is where technology goes) × 10 K peasants per unit; peasants past it make `global.subsistence_marginal_yield` (0.5) of the yield (Q25), and `subsistence_farm_output_factor` from ideas and laws scales the yield (Q94). The upper class takes `global.subsistence_rent_share` (0.2) as rent (Q27). Peasants pay base prices for the share of their basket they grow, and market prices for the 20% they buy (Q92, in `get_pop_needs`), which took them off wealth 1. World subsistence output is within 1% of M04's.
- **Natural resources (M06):** deposits are state history data (see Data model), and HOI4's `resources` blocks no longer feed the economy; the army still gets its goods through `resource_trade`. Content's 535 vanilla `add_resource` calls were converted by script: natural goods to `add_x_deposit` / `remove_x_deposit`, one for one (Q96), and manufactured goods to their factory through `add_x_industries`, at the same jobs (Q97). Building an RGO needs known deposits nobody works yet (`can_build_industry`). Prospecting (Q99) is the state screen's government-paid search: a find moves 10–30% of the remaining potential into known deposits (times prospecting ability), and a major find 50–100%; nothing is found from nothing. Startup no longer rolls random potential (Q100). The prospect button hides while known deposits wait to be worked, so until M09 a state finds each good once.

Transport supply from population, after roads, is capped at `global.base_infrastructure_cap` (40), raised by `base_infrastructure_cap_modifier` and then `base_infrastructure_cap_factor`, which nothing grants yet (Q103).
